Consider at least:

- what makes two references denote the same entity;
- what persists or changes across time, state, location, ownership, and version;
- rigid types versus roles, phases, states, and contextual classifications;
- dependence, constitution, participation, membership, and part-whole distinctions;
- granularity and intentionally ignored distinctions;
- 3D/4D, universal/particular, descriptive/prescriptive, or other foundational choices when material;
- open-world, local closed-world, distinctness, uniqueness, and completeness boundaries;
- validity time, transaction time, jurisdiction, source, authority, and conflicting viewpoints;
- relation arity, direction, characteristics, exceptions, and qualification needs;
- who may resolve conflicts and how the decision is recorded.

## Pressure tests

For each material commitment, record:

- a positive example that must fit;
- a counterexample or boundary case that must not be misclassified;
- the expected entailment or non-entailment;
- the CQ, SHACL shape, SPARQL query, or expert decision that verifies it;
- the migration consequence if the commitment changes.
